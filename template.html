Traffic control on 9th Street, Lincoln

A MaleCNS neural circuit trained to select signal phases for a hypothetical post-game traffic pulse. The study uses three actual signal locations—9th & Q, 9th & P, and 9th & O—with synthetic demand and simplified vehicle queues.

1. Neural circuit

The model retains 196 individual neurons and 2,966 directed connections from a small patch of the male fly’s right optic lobe. Synapse counts initialize connection strength; training adjusts their magnitudes while preserving the measured graph and assumed excitatory or inhibitory signs.

4. Training procedure

Supervised imitation of a downstream-aware queue heuristic. The target favors the larger effective queue, accounts for downstream congestion, and adds a small preference for keeping the current phase. This experiment does not use reinforcement learning.

Training data21,600 state–action examples from 90 simulated episodes; 4,320 validation examples from 18 separate episodes. Teacher, fixed, and random behavior policies supply a range of queue states.
Optimization30 epochs; Adam, learning rate 0.003; batch size 256; binary cross-entropy; gradient norm clipped at 1. Lowest validation loss selects each checkpoint.
Parameters3,819 for the measured and rewired circuits. The conventional two-hidden-layer network has 5,379 parameters and is a practical reference, not a matched-size control.
Rewiring controlPreserves each neuron’s incoming and outgoing degree, cell-type pairings, connection count, and source-associated synapse-count multiset. Spatial wiring and weighted incoming sums change.
InitializationTrained from scratch using the same circuit as the earlier motion experiment. Motion-classifier weights are not transferred.

hk+1 = (1 − α)hk + α tanh(Whk + Bu + b)

u is the traffic observation; B drives the L1/L3 neurons; W follows measured connections. Eight updates start from zero for each decision. A linear decoder reads T4 states and requests three binary phases. These signed states and learned time constants are artificial dynamics, not measured firing rates.

5. Assumptions and limits

The model has two movement groups per junction, a 10-second time step, an assumed discharge limit of eight vehicles per active movement per step, and a 60-vehicle downstream receiving threshold. A phase change loses one step; the shared guard permits at least 20 seconds of green and limits it to 80 seconds when conflicting traffic is waiting.

Actual arrival counts, turning movements, lane geometry, pedestrian flows, police direction, present game-day closures, and Lincoln’s signal timings are not represented. Vehicle transfer between adjacent queues takes one step and is not calibrated from distance. Exogenous queues may grow without bound; vehicles are conserved rather than discarded.

This is a reproducible control experiment using Lincoln locations. Its reported reductions are simulation results, not forecasts of game-day travel time or recommendations for operating city signals.
